8 Real Photographers & Studios — What Their Sites Do

A build reference for your own portfolio site. Eight working professionals across different niches, each with URL, sitemap, verbatim hero copy, gallery structure, and features. No template galleries — every site below belongs to a real practising photographer or studio.

Compiled 21 July 2026 · sourced from live homepages & curated 2026 roundups

How to read the hero copy: quotes are transcribed from each live homepage. Several of these sites are deliberately image-led and carry no on-page hero sentence — for those the exact page title / meta line is given and flagged, because that emptiness is itself the design choice worth noting.

6 patterns that show up across all 8 sites

Not "pick one of three approaches" — these are the traits every single site in the set shares, checked individually against all eight. If a good photography portfolio does nothing else, it does these.

1 · One name, one identity — never a marketplace template

Every site runs on the photographer's own name as the domain, with no agency branding or template chrome around it. The identity is the brand, from the URL down.

levonbiss.commpkelley.comporodina.competermckinnon.comjonathangregson.co.ukamylombard.comcassandraladru.comscottsnyderphoto.com

2 · The work is always grouped by a deliberate taxonomy — never a flat stream

No site dumps every photo into one undifferentiated grid. Each picks a single organizing idea for the whole site: named series, project/case-study, subject or genre, or story-by-couple. The taxonomy differs by niche, but having one at all does not.

Biss → named seriesKelley → project/commissionPorodina → client/publicationMcKinnon → subject typeGregson → client & projectLombard → genre filtersLadru → by coupleSnyder → case study

3 · Hero copy is short and plain, or skipped entirely — never marketing fluff

Across all eight, the opening line tops out at a couple of plain, factual sentences (Ladru's is the longest in the set, and it's still two sentences of description, not paragraphs of ad copy). Two of eight run zero hero text and let a full-bleed image open cold. Nobody is selling with adjectives up top.

Kelley → nonePorodina → noneBiss → tagline onlyMcKinnon → 3 wordsSnyder → 3 words + 1 sentenceGregson → title + 1 sentenceLombard → 1 sentenceLadru → 2 sentences

4 · Credibility is shown through names, not adjectives

Every site places some form of third-party proof directly against the work — client logos, press mentions, named landmark projects, exhibition history, or testimonials — so recognizable brands and publications do the persuading instead of self-description.

Biss → exhibitionsKelley → landmark projectsPorodina → client tile wallMcKinnon → Clients pageGregson → client roster + agentLombard → client listLadru → press wall + testimonialsSnyder → client wall + testimonials

5 · Instagram + a direct contact path are the two non-negotiable utilities

Regardless of niche or how sparse the rest of the nav is — even Scott Snyder's Work + Contact, even Porodina's thumbnails + Contacts — every one of the eight ships both an Instagram link and a direct way to get in touch.

BissKelleyPorodinaMcKinnonGregsonLombardLadruSnyder

6 · No blog, no journal — the site stays portfolio + conversion

None of the eight run a text-content or news section. There's no SEO-article layer competing with the images; the site's only jobs are showing the work and capturing an inquiry (explicitly confirmed absent for Porodina; absent by omission everywhere else in the set).
